Who is getting relief, and how

Automatic expunction swamps everything else when it is running, and drops to zero when the legislature pauses it. Petition-based relief, especially for convictions, moves slowly.

The conviction gap

Clearing dismissals is now largely automatic. Clearing convictions still requires a petition, a $175 fee, a hearing, and a waiting period, and very few eligible people get through it.

At the FY 2024–25 rate, granting relief to everyone already estimated eligible would take about … years, before counting anyone who becomes eligible in the meantime. The eligibility estimate (Santa Clara Law's Paper Prisons project) modeled pre-2020 rules from a 2017–18 sample of 2,492 criminal histories; the Second Chance Act's expansions likely make the true number higher. Refreshing this estimate with current law and AOC court data is the most important upgrade for this dashboard.

Second Chance Act implementation

The law passed unanimously in 2020. Its automatic provisions have spent nearly as long paused as running.

    What this dashboard needs next

    The public reports give statewide counts by statute only. The questions SCSJ most needs answered (who is left behind, and where) require more granular data.
